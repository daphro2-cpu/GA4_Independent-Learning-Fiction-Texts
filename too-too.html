Part 2: Prediction

Reading Strategy: Prediction

Good readers make predictions before and during reading. A prediction is a smart guess based on the title, story clues, and what we already know.

Predictions help us focus on possible problems, clues, and character actions.

Look at the Clues

Clue 1

The title is Too-Too the Listener.
Clue 2

Listening carefully is very important in the story.
Clue 3

There may be a mystery or problem that needs clues.

Which prediction is BEST?

Too-Too will learn how to cook.
Too-Too may solve a problem by listening carefully and noticing clues.
The story will mostly explain farming tools.

Part 3: Reading Skill Prep — Inference

Reading Skill: Inference

An inference is a smart idea readers figure out using clues and evidence from the story.

The answer is not always directly stated.

Good readers ask:
  • What clues do I notice?
  • What does the character say or do?
  • What does this probably mean?

Practice

Clue: A character keeps listening carefully instead of speaking quickly.

What can readers infer?

The character dislikes all people.
The character believes listening carefully helps understand the problem.
The character wants to sleep all day.

Now Read the Story

Read Too-Too the Listener. While reading, look for:
  • important clues
  • what characters say
  • what characters do
  • details that help solve the mystery/problem
  • evidence that supports your ideas

Part 4: After-Reading Quick Check

After Reading Strategy: Check the Clues

After reading, good readers look back at the clues, the character actions, and the evidence.

For this story, ask: What clues mattered most? What can readers infer? What evidence supports the answer?

Part 5: Character Clues

Readers learn about characters from:
  • what they say
  • what they do
  • how they react
  • how other characters respond to them

The worksheet may ask you to explain:
  • what a clue means
  • what readers can infer
  • what evidence supports the idea

Choose one clue and one matching inference.

Clues

Characters are confused by the mystery/problem.

Inference

The problem is difficult and needs careful thinking.
Paying attention carefully can help solve problems.

Part 7: Text Evidence Practice

Text evidence means using clues from the story to support your answer.

Strong answers use:
  • a clear idea
  • a story clue
  • an explanation

Which answer uses the BEST evidence?

Too-Too is smart because I think so.
Too-Too listens carefully and notices clues that other characters miss. This evidence shows Too-Too solves problems by paying attention.
Too-Too likes stories and food.

Part 8: Worksheet Preparation

Your worksheet may ask you to:
  • make an inference
  • identify clues
  • explain character behaviour
  • use text evidence
A strong worksheet answer should:
  • answer the question clearly
  • use a clue from the story
  • explain what the clue means

Choose the strongest worksheet answer.

Too-Too is nice. The story is interesting.
Too-Too solves problems by listening carefully and noticing clues. For example, Too-Too pays attention to details that other characters ignore.
Too-Too never thinks carefully.
