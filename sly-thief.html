Part 2: Prediction

Reading Strategy: Prediction

Good readers make predictions before and during reading. A prediction is a smart guess based on the title, pictures, story clues, and what we already know.

Prediction helps us focus on the problem and think about how the characters may try to solve it.

Look at the Clues

Clue 1

The title is The Sly Thief.
Clue 2

A thief uses tricks to steal things.
Clue 3

The king and his people need to catch the thief.

Which prediction is BEST?

The thief will teach everyone how to farm.
The thief may use clever tricks, and the king’s people will need a clever plan to catch him.
The story will mostly explain how to cook food.

Part 3: Reading Skill Prep — Problem & Solution

Reading Skill: Problem & Solution

A problem is something that needs to be fixed. A solution is what characters do to try to fix the problem.

In a trickster or clever story, the solution often needs careful thinking. Good readers ask:
  • What is the problem?
  • Who is trying to solve it?
  • What plan do they use?
  • Does the plan work?

Practice

Problem: A thief keeps stealing from the king.

Which solution makes the most sense?

The king ignores the thief forever.
The king’s people make a clever plan to catch the thief.
The thief receives a prize for stealing.

Now Read the Story

Read The Sly Thief. While reading, look for:
  • what the thief steals
  • how the thief tricks people
  • what problem the king has
  • what plan is used to catch the thief
  • whether the solution works

Part 4: After-Reading Quick Check

After Reading Strategy: Check and Think Again

After we read, good readers check the order of events and the problem-solution structure. For this story, ask: What did the thief do?, how did people respond?, and what clever solution helped solve the problem?

Part 5: Sequence Practice

Choose the event that happened at each point in the story.

1. What is the main problem?

2. What happens as the story continues?

3. What kind of solution is needed?

Part 6: Problem → Solution Match

The worksheet may ask you to identify the problem, the attempted solution, and the final result. Matching these ideas helps you prepare stronger answers.

Choose one problem/action and one matching solution/result.

Problem / Action

The thief steals from the king.
The thief uses tricks and lies.
The king wants the stealing to stop.

Solution / Result

People need to think carefully instead of trusting too quickly.
A clever plan is needed to catch the thief.
This creates the main problem of the story.

Part 7: Clever Thinking Practice

Clever thinking means solving a problem by noticing details, making a plan, and not being tricked easily.

Which character action shows clever thinking?

Believing everything the thief says without checking.
Making a plan that uses the thief’s own tricks against him.
Giving the thief more chances to steal.

Part 8: Worksheet Preparation

Your worksheet may ask you to explain the problem, the solution, and how clever thinking helped. A strong answer should include:
  • the problem
  • the solution or plan
  • why the plan worked
  • a clue from the story

Choose the strongest worksheet answer.

The thief was sly. The story was interesting.
The main problem was that the thief used tricks to steal. The solution needed clever thinking because the characters had to notice the thief’s tricks and make a plan to stop him.
The king should let the thief keep stealing because he is clever.
