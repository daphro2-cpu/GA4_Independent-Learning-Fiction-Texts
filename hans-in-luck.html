Part 2: Prediction

Reading Strategy: Prediction

Good readers make predictions using the title, story clues, and what they already know. Predictions help readers focus on what may happen next.

In this story, think about whether Hans’s choices will make him more lucky, less lucky, or simply happier.

Look at the Clues

Clue 1

The title is Hans in Luck.
Clue 2

Hans keeps trading one thing for another.
Clue 3

A trade can look bad to one person but good to another.

Which prediction is BEST?

Hans will never make any choices.
Hans may make trades that look foolish, but he may still feel lucky or happy.
The story will mostly explain how to build houses.

Part 3: Reading Skill Prep — Sequence + Value Judgement

Reading Skill: Sequence

Sequence means the order that events happen in a story. In this story, you need to follow Hans’s trades carefully.

Value judgement means deciding whether something is good, bad, helpful, harmful, wise, or foolish — and explaining why.

Good readers ask:
  • What did Hans have first?
  • What did he trade it for?
  • Was the trade good or bad?
  • Why did Hans feel happy about it?

Practice

Situation: Hans trades something valuable for something easier to carry.

Which is the best value judgement?

It must be good for everyone because all trades are good.
It may be bad because he loses value, but good from Hans’s view because it feels easier for him.
It has no effect on Hans at all.

Now Read the Story

Read Hans in Luck. While reading, look for:
  • what Hans has at the beginning
  • what he trades each thing for
  • why he agrees to each trade
  • whether the trade seems good or bad
  • why Hans feels lucky or happy

Part 4: After-Reading Quick Check

After Reading Strategy: Check the Pattern

After reading, good readers check the pattern of the story. For this story, ask: What did Hans trade?, why did he trade it?, and how did Hans feel after each trade?

Part 5: Sequence Practice

Choose the event that happened at each point in the story.

1. What happens first?

Hans begins with something valuable after working for a long time.
Hans starts with nothing and finds treasure.
Hans immediately returns home without making any trades.

2. What happens again and again?

Hans refuses to trade anything.
Hans trades what he has for something else and feels pleased with the change.
Hans becomes angry after every trade.

3. What is important to understand at the end?

Hans clearly becomes richer and richer.
Hans learns to stop helping people.
Hans may have lost valuable things, but he still feels free and happy.

Part 6: Trade → Result Match

The worksheet may ask you to follow Hans’s choices and explain whether his trades were good or bad. Matching trades with results helps you prepare stronger answers.

Choose one trade idea and one matching result.

Trade / Choice

Hans trades something valuable for something easier to manage.
Hans keeps agreeing to new trades.
Hans ends with much less than he had before.

Result / Meaning

The story creates a pattern of repeated choices and changes.
Readers must decide whether Hans is foolish, lucky, or happy in his own way.
The trade may lower the value, but it may feel better to Hans.

Part 7: Good or Bad Point?

Some choices can have both good and bad points. A strong reader explains both sides before making a judgement.

Which answer gives both sides?

Every trade Hans makes is completely perfect.
Hans’s trades may be bad because he loses value, but good for him because he feels lighter, freer, or happier.
Hans’s choices do not matter in the story.

Part 8: Theme Practice

A theme is the lesson or message of a story. In this story, the lesson may depend on how we understand Hans’s happiness.

Which theme BEST fits this story?

Having more expensive things always makes people happier.
Happiness can depend on how a person sees their situation, not only on what they own.
People should never travel home.

Part 9: Worksheet Preparation

Your worksheet may ask you to explain Hans’s choices, sequence the trades, or decide whether Hans was truly lucky. A strong answer should include:
  • a clear opinion
  • one story example
  • an explanation of why the example supports your idea

Choose the strongest worksheet answer.

Hans is lucky because the title says luck.
Hans may seem foolish because he loses valuable things, but he feels lucky because each trade makes him feel happier or freer. This shows that Hans values comfort and happiness more than money.
Hans is unlucky because everyone else is bad.
