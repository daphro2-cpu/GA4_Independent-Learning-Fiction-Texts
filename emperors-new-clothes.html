Part 2: Prediction

Reading Strategy: Prediction

Good readers make predictions before and during reading. A prediction is a smart guess based on the title, pictures, story clues, and what we already know.

Prediction helps us focus on the characters, the problem, and what may happen next.

Look at the Clues

Clue 1

The title is The Emperor’s New Clothes.
Clue 2

The emperor cares a lot about beautiful clothes.
Clue 3

Some people may be afraid to say what they really think.

Which prediction is BEST?

The emperor will become a farmer and grow crops.
The emperor may be tricked because he cares too much about clothes and what others think.
The story will only explain how to sew normal clothes.

Part 3: Reading Skill Prep — Character Motivation

Reading Skill: Character Motivation

A character’s motivation is the reason why the character says or does something. Sometimes the reason is directly stated. Sometimes we infer it from actions, reactions, and clues.

Good readers ask:
  • What does this character want?
  • What is this character afraid of?
  • Why does the character pretend or stay quiet?
  • What clue supports my idea?

Practice

Text clue: A person cannot see the cloth, but says it is beautiful.

Why might the person say this?

The person wants to eat the cloth.
The person is afraid others will think they are foolish or unfit for their job.
The person wants to sleep.

Now Read the Story

Read The Emperor’s New Clothes. While reading, look for:
  • what the weavers claim about the cloth
  • why the emperor believes them
  • why the officials pretend to see the cloth
  • who finally tells the truth
  • what lesson the story teaches

Part 4: After-Reading Quick Check

After Reading Strategy: Check and Think Again

After we read, good readers check what happened and why it happened. For this story, ask: Who was tricked?, why did people pretend?, and what lesson can readers learn?

Part 5: Character Reaction → Reason

The worksheet may ask you to explain character reactions and support your ideas with evidence. Match what a character does with the best reason.

Choose one reaction and one matching reason.

Character Reactions

The emperor wants the special cloth.
The officials say the cloth is beautiful even though they cannot see it.
A child says the emperor has no clothes.

Reasons

They are afraid people will think they are foolish or bad at their jobs.
The child is honest and says what everyone can see.
He loves fine clothing and wants to seem wise and important.

Part 6: Evidence Practice

Choose the best evidence for each idea.

1. Which evidence shows the emperor is proud?

The child tells the truth.
The emperor cares a lot about having wonderful clothes.
The people walk in the street.

2. Which evidence shows people are afraid to tell the truth?

The emperor walks in a parade.
The weavers ask for fine materials.
People pretend to see the cloth because they do not want to look foolish.

3. Which evidence shows honesty?

The child says the emperor is not wearing any clothes.
The officials praise the cloth.
The weavers pretend to work.

Part 7: Theme Practice

A theme is the message or lesson in a story. A theme is not just what happened. It explains what the story teaches us about people, choices, or life.

Which theme best fits this story?

Expensive clothes are always the most important thing.
People should be honest instead of pretending because they are afraid of what others think.
Children should never speak in public.

Part 8: Worksheet Preparation

Your worksheet may ask you to explain why characters acted the way they did, how they reacted, or what lesson the story teaches. A strong answer should include:
  • a clear idea
  • one story clue
  • an explanation of what the clue shows

Choose the strongest worksheet answer.

The emperor likes clothes. The story is funny.
The story teaches that people should be honest. Many people pretended to see the cloth because they were afraid of looking foolish, but the child told the truth.
The weavers made good clothes, and everyone liked them.
