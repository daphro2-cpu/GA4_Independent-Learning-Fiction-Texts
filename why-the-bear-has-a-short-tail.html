Part 2: Prediction

Reading Strategy: Prediction

Good readers make predictions before and during reading. We use the title, story clues, and what we know about folktales.

This title explains why something is true today. That means the story will probably show how Bear’s tail became short.

Look at the Clues

Clue 1

The title is Why the Bear Has a Short Tail.
Clue 2

Fox is often a trickster character in folktales.
Clue 3

Bear may trust Fox too easily.

Which prediction is BEST?

Bear will teach Fox how to build a house.
Fox may trick Bear, and the trick may explain why Bear has a short tail.
The story will mostly explain how birds fly.

Part 3: Reading Skill Prep — Cause & Effect

Reading Skill: Cause & Effect

A cause is why something happens. An effect is what happens because of it.

In this story, one trick causes a big result. Good readers ask:
  • What does Fox tell Bear to do?
  • Why does Bear believe Fox?
  • What happens because Bear follows Fox’s advice?
  • What does this explain?

Practice

Cause: Bear trusts Fox’s strange fishing advice.

What is the most likely effect?

Bear immediately becomes wiser than Fox.
Bear may get into trouble because Fox is tricking him.
Fox forgets how to trick people.

Now Read the Story

Read Why the Bear Has a Short Tail. While reading, look for:
  • what Fox wants
  • what Fox tells Bear
  • why Bear believes Fox
  • what happens to Bear’s tail
  • what lesson the story teaches

Part 4: After-Reading Quick Check

After Reading Strategy: Check the Cause Chain

After reading, good readers check how one event leads to the next. For this story, ask: What did Fox do?, what did Bear do?, and what happened because of it?

Part 5: Sequence Practice

Choose the event that happened at each point in the story.

1. What happens first?

Fox tricks Bear with advice about catching fish.
Bear already has a short tail before anything happens.
Bear teaches Fox a lesson.

2. What happens next?

Bear ignores Fox and walks away.
Bear follows Fox’s advice and waits in the cold.
Fox gives Bear a new tail.

3. What is the result?

Fox becomes Bear’s best helper.
Bear catches hundreds of fish with no problem.
Bear’s tail is hurt or broken, which explains why bears have short tails.

Part 6: Cause → Effect Match

The worksheet may ask you to explain why Bear has a short tail. Matching causes and effects helps you prepare a stronger answer.

Choose one cause and one matching effect.

Cause

Fox wants to trick Bear.
Bear believes Fox’s advice.
Bear leaves his tail in the cold for too long.

Effect

Bear follows the foolish fishing method.
Bear’s tail is damaged and becomes short.
Fox gives Bear false advice.

Part 7: Trickster Character Practice

A trickster character uses cleverness, tricks, or lies to get what they want. In this story, Fox is the trickster.

Which answer BEST describes Fox?

Fox is honest and only wants to help Bear.
Fox is clever but dishonest because he tricks Bear with bad advice.
Fox is careless because he never makes a plan.

Part 8: Theme Practice

A theme is the lesson or message of a story. In trickster tales, the lesson often teaches readers to think carefully before trusting someone.

Which theme BEST fits this story?

Bears should always believe everything foxes say.
People should think carefully before trusting advice, especially from someone who may trick them.
Fishing is always easy in winter.

Part 9: Worksheet Preparation

Your worksheet may ask you to explain why Bear has a short tail, describe Fox’s trick, or explain the lesson of the story. A strong answer should include:
  • a clear answer
  • one story event as evidence
  • an explanation of how the event caused the result

Choose the strongest worksheet answer.

Bear has a short tail because the title says so.
Bear has a short tail because Fox tricked him into using a foolish fishing method. Bear trusted Fox, stayed in the cold too long, and his tail was damaged.
Fox helped Bear catch many fish, so Bear was happy.
