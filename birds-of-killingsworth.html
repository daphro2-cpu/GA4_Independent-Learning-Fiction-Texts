Part 2: Prediction

Reading Strategy: Prediction

Good readers make predictions before and during reading. A prediction is a smart guess based on the title, pictures, story clues, and what we already know.

Prediction helps us focus on the story problem and check whether our thinking changes as we read.

Look at the Clues

Clue 1

The title is The Birds of Killingsworth.
Clue 2

The farmers think the birds will eat their crops.
Clue 3

Birds also eat insects that can harm plants.

Which prediction is BEST?

The birds will teach the farmers how to sing.
The people may learn that the birds are not only a problem; they may also be helpful.
The story will mostly explain how to build bird nests.

Part 3: Reading Skill Prep — Problem & Solution

Reading Skill: Problem & Solution

A problem is something that needs to be fixed. A solution is what people do to try to fix the problem.

But sometimes a solution creates a new problem. Good readers ask:
  • What was the problem?
  • Who was affected by the problem?
  • What did the characters do to solve it?
  • What was the result?

Practice

Problem: The farmers believe the birds will eat their crops.

Which solution do the farmers choose?

They plant more flowers for the birds.
They chase the birds away from the town.
They build bigger nests for the birds.

Now Read the Story

Read The Birds of Killingsworth. While reading, look for:
  • what the townspeople think the problem is
  • who is happy and who is not happy
  • what the farmers do to solve the problem
  • what happens after the birds leave
  • what the people realise at the end

Part 4: After-Reading Quick Check

After Reading Strategy: Check and Think Again

After we read, good readers check the problem, the solution, and the result. We also think about different points of view: who was happy, who was unhappy, and why.

Part 5: Problem → Solution → Result

1. What was the problem?

2. What was done to solve the problem?

3. What was the result?

Part 6: Different Points of View

The worksheet asks who was happy and who was not happy. To answer well, think about what each group wanted.

Choose one group and the matching reason.

Groups

The children
The farmers at first
The town after the birds came back

Reasons

They thought the birds would destroy their crops.
They understood the birds helped protect the crops from insects.
They loved the birds and wanted to protect them.

Part 7: Worksheet Preparation

Your worksheet asks you to explain the problem, solution, result, and whether everyone was happy at the end. A strong answer should include:
  • what happened
  • who felt happy or unhappy
  • why they felt that way
  • a clue from the story

Choose the strongest worksheet answer.

Everyone was happy because birds are nice.
At the end, the town was happy because the people realised the birds helped stop insects from damaging the crops. The children were also happy because the birds returned.
The farmers were right because birds should never live near crops.
